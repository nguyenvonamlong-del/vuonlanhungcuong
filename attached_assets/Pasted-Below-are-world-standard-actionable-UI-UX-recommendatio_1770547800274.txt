## 1) Information architecture that feels “premium”

### A. Make “what to do next” obvious everywhere

**Rule:** every page must have *one* dominant primary action (CTA) and at most *one* secondary action.

* **Shop (`/shop`)**: Primary = “View details” or “Add to cart” (pick one as primary depending on your strategy). Secondary = the other. Don’t make both equal weight. 
* **Checkout (`/checkout`)**: Primary = “Continue” / “Place order”; secondary = “Back”. Never introduce a third CTA in the step footer. 
* **Admin Orders (`/dashboard/orders`)**: Primary = “Update status” or “Assign technician” depending on row state; hide non-essential actions in a kebab menu.

### B. Add “Saved Views” for pro workflows (Admin tables)

In every management table (Orders, Inventory, Customers, Technicians, Suppliers, Audit Log), add:

* Saved filters (e.g., “Delayed shipments”, “VIP urgent queue”, “Low stock orchids”)
* Column visibility + order
* Default sort per view
  This is one of the biggest deltas between “amateur” and “prestige” internal tools: **operators feel in control**.

## 2) Visual hierarchy: premium = calmer, clearer, fewer competing signals

Your design system is already structured (tokens, typography levels, flat design). The prestige jump comes from **stricter hierarchy enforcement**:

### A. Enforce a “3-line card rule” (public product cards)

On product cards, cap the visible text to:

1. Product name
2. One-line composition summary (e.g., “3× Pink 3.5 + Driftwood”)
3. Total price + delivery estimate line (if available)

Everything else goes into the detail dialog. This prevents “accounting UI” from leaking into shopping UX.

### B. Replace “dense cost breakdown” with progressive disclosure

Show:

* **Total** prominently
* “Includes: orchids, pot, decor” as a single line
* A “View breakdown” accordion inside detail dialog, not on the grid

## 3) Product discovery on `/shop`: make it feel like a flagship store

### A. Filters: show impact and reduce uncertainty

Add these behaviors:

* **Result count updates** as filters change (e.g., “24 items”)
* **Disable impossible filter options** (with tooltip “No items match”)
* **Sticky filter summary bar** on scroll: “Pink • 3.5 • Bat Trang (Clear all)”

### B. Search that behaves like a premium catalog

* Autocomplete with categories: *Orchid Types*, *Pot Styles*, *Decorations*
* Typo tolerance (“hồ điệp” vs “ho diep”)
* “Did you mean … ?” for common VN input variations (tone marks)

---

## 4) Checkout `/checkout`: remove friction, increase trust (VN payment reality)

Your flow includes multi-step checkout. 
To make it world-class:

### A. Address UX: VN-specific validation

* Province/District/Ward dropdowns should be **searchable**, with caching and “recent addresses”
* Auto-format phone numbers; show carrier icon if detected
* Inline “Delivery restrictions” (if any) per province

### B. Shipping selection must feel deterministic

For each shipping option card:

* **Guaranteed date range** (e.g., “Arrives Tue–Wed”)
* Cutoff time (“Order before 12:00 for same-day dispatch” if applicable)
* A “Why this cost?” tooltip (distance, handling, fragility)

### C. VietQR / bank transfer: add trust scaffolding

If you show VietQR + proof upload:

* Display **bank name, account name, last-4**, and a copy button for each field
* Show a “Payment status timeline” (Submitted proof → Verified → Deposit received)
* After upload, show **checksum-like confirmation**: “We detected transfer amount: ___ (edit if wrong)”

This dramatically reduces support tickets.

## 5) Order tracking `/tracking`: premium = proactive + self-serve

The tracking page exists in your IA. 

Add:

* A **single “tracking token” entry** as default; phone/email lookup as secondary
* Status timeline with **timestamps** and “What happens next” copy per status
* A “Need help?” panel with context-aware quick actions:

  * “Change delivery time”
  * “Update address” (policy-based)
  * “Report damaged item” (starts a Return/Exception flow)

---

## 6) Customer notifications: make it feel premium, not spammy

Create a **Notification Center UX spec**:

* Every order status change must map to:

  * Customer-facing message
  * Channel priority (push/email/SMS/voice for urgent)
  * Quiet hours rules
  * Retry & fallback logic
* Customer can opt into “VIP updates” (more granular) vs “Essential only”

**Key prestige detail:** messages must answer:

1. What changed
2. Why it matters
3. What you need from the customer (if anything)
4. Next expected timestamp

---

## 7) Technician + queue UX: “ops-grade” controls

To make the assembly workflow feel like a modern fulfillment system:

* Add a **Queue Board** (Kanban + SLA timers) with:

  * VIP/Urgent pinned lane
  * “Aging” indicator (time in status)
  * Blocking reasons (missing pot, missing orchid type, payment pending)
* Technician profile page:

  * Current WIP count
  * Skill tags (orchid types, complex decor)
  * Availability forecast (next 2–3 hours / today)
* Assignment UI:

  * “Suggested technician” (based on load + skill + SLA)
  * One-click assign + immediate toast + audit entry

---

## 8) Chatbot ordering: prestige = controlled + transparent

You want guest checkout via chatbot + normal UI. Your doc mentions chatbot modes. 

Make it premium by enforcing:

* A **side-by-side “Order Draft” panel** while chatting (always visible)
* “Confirm” gates for money steps:

  * Shipping choice confirmation
  * Address confirmation
  * Deposit amount confirmation
* Explicit **handoff to human**:

  * “Talk to staff now” with expected response time
* Clear privacy statement for guest users (what you store, for how long)

---

## 9) i18n (VI/EN): premium = not just translation

To support Vietnamese + English well:

* Use **locale-aware formatting**:

  * VND formatting, separators, dates, times
* Avoid text expansion breakage:

  * English strings are often longer; define max widths + truncation rules
* Maintain **translation keys for statuses** (customer-facing vs internal ops might differ)
